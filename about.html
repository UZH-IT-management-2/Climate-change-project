About the explorer

Making six decades of Swiss climate data visible.

Swiss Shift is a neutral, interactive view of annual climate observations published by MeteoSwiss. It is an independent university project and is not an official federal service.

Open the climate map →
01

What you can explore

The map presents annual mean temperature, precipitation, relative sunshine duration, and hot-day counts. Use the timeline to compare years from 1961 through the latest complete year shared by the datasets.

02

Data sources

Gridded values come from the MeteoSwiss Open Data spatial climate analyses. Hot-day markers use homogenized annual observations from the Swiss National Basic Climatological Network (NBCN).

MeteoSwiss Open Data documentation ↗

03

The 1961–1990 baseline

For every compatible grid cell or station, the baseline is the arithmetic mean of actual observations from 1961 through 1990. Difference mode subtracts that baseline from the selected year in the original unit.

selected year − 1961–1990 mean
04

Processing method

A reproducible Python pipeline discovers annual files through the official STAC catalogue, reads NetCDF grids, respects Swiss LV95 (EPSG:2056), reprojects them to WGS84, preserves missing values, computes baselines, and exports fixed-scale transparent PNG overlays and lookup grids.

05

Limitations and uncertainty

Grids are statistical spatial analyses rather than direct measurements at every pixel. Sunshine uses MeteoSwiss’s reconstructed annual series for complete baseline coverage. Homogenized daily-derived indices can carry more uncertainty than monthly observations. Raster popups use a compact sampled lookup grid and are rounded.

06

Reading the map

Each variable uses one fixed scale across every year, so colors remain comparable through time. In difference mode, the neutral midpoint represents no difference from the historical baseline.

08

Privacy

No analytics, advertising, cookies, accounts, or personal database are used. On first load, one request to ipwho.is estimates only whether the visitor is in Switzerland so the map can choose an initial view. The application does not store the IP address, and the map still works if the request fails.
